Capstone Project Guidelines and Evaluation Rubrics 
The Capstone Project is a culminating assignment undertaken by students in the final year of the programme, allowing 
them to apply the knowledge and skills gained over previous years of study. Students are expected to design, develop, 
and evaluate solutions to complex engineering problems using appropriate technology, tools, and engineering practice. 
1. Programme Outcomes Addressed 
● 
Think critically and solve challenging, open-ended problems by designing innovative solutions using acquired 
technical expertise. 
● 
Apply engineering knowledge to real-world problems through innovative project development. 
● 
Design and implement systems, components, or prototypes using modern tools, techniques, and 
interdisciplinary skills. 
● 
Demonstrate effective teamwork, leadership, and project management abilities. 
● 
Communicate technical information clearly and professionally through documentation and presentations. 
● 
Adhere to professional and ethical standards, giving due consideration to societal, environmental, and 
sustainability aspects. 
 
 
2. Procedure and Roles 
1. Students are formed into batches of 3–5 members from the same specialization, working under the guidance of an 
assigned project guide. 
2. Each section/class is assigned a dedicated Section In-Charge, responsible for monitoring the progress of all projects 
within that section. 
3. Each department constitutes an Internal Review Panel comprising the Project Guide, a Mentor, and one or two 
additional faculty members with relevant domain expertise. 
4. The evaluation process comprises three internal reviews (Review 1, 2, and 3) followed by one Final End-Semester 
External Review. Each internal review is assessed through three components: Continuous Evaluation, Guide Evaluation, 
and a Panel/Project Review — described in detail in Section 3. 
5. Continuous Evaluation is conducted through in-session presentations delivered by students during scheduled project 
hours, at a minimum frequency of two presentations per month per batch. 
Note: Faculty must upload marks to the LMS immediately after each session or review. Each batch is responsible for meeting its 
project guide at least twice a week and ensuring the guide records evaluation marks in the LMS accordingly. Failure to comply with 
these timelines may result in loss of continuous evaluation marks.

3.1 How Component Scores Combine (Scaling Formulas) 
Each review is assessed using rubrics scored out of 50 marks (Sections 6–8) or 100 marks (Section 9). These raw scores 
are then scaled down to fit the weightage shown above, as follows: 
Scaling formula:  Continuous Evaluation (per review) = (Average of all session scores in that review period ÷ 50) × 5 
Scaling formula:  Guide Evaluation (per review) = (Guide's Individual Evaluation Sheet score ÷ 50) × 5 
Scaling formula:  Panel / Project Review (per review) = (Panel Evaluation Rubric score ÷ 50) × 10 
Scaling formula:  Final External Review contribution = (End-Semester External Review score ÷ 100) × 40 
These formulas allow every rubric to be filled out on its natural 0–50 or 0–100 scale during the review, while still rolling up 
consistently into the 100-mark overall total.

4. General Review Guidelines (Applicable to All Reviews) 
Guide Presence is Mandatory: The internal project guide must be present at every review, as their evaluation and 
professional insight are integral to the process. 
Panel Constitution: Each review panel must include the Project Guide and one or more Subject Experts / Review 
Committee Members. 
Adherence to Schedule: Reviews must be conducted as per the department's academic calendar. Delayed reviews 
without prior approval may result in deduction of marks. 
Pre-Submission of Documents: Students must submit all required documents (e.g., abstract, review report, slides) at 
least 2 days before the scheduled review date. 
Attendance is Compulsory: All group members must be physically present. Absence without a valid, pre-approved 
reason will affect marks and may result in failure of the capstone project for that member. 
Time Allocation: Each group is allotted 10–15 minutes to present, followed by 5–10 minutes for questions and 
discussion. 
Academic Integrity: All content presented — including reports, code, and presentations — must be original. Plagiarism 
or misrepresentation of work will result in severe academic consequences, up to and including disqualification from the 
review. Departments are encouraged to run submitted reports through a plagiarism-detection tool prior to the review. 
Post-Review Responsibilities 
● 
Feedback must be recorded and shared with students after every review. 
● 
Marks must be documented and signed by the guide and all panel members before submission. 
● 
If a project is found incomplete or not meeting academic standards, a re-review must be scheduled within a 
timeframe set by the department.

9. Final End-Semester External Review 
Pre-Review Preparations 
Guide Presence is Mandatory: the final review must not be conducted without the internal project guide's presence. 
Document Submission (2–3 days before the review): 
● 
Final project report (spiral/hardbound, signed by the guide) 
● 
Presentation slides, as per department format 
● 
User manual / supporting documents, if applicable 
● 
Project demonstration link / code repository, if applicable 
Project Display Setup: ensure laptops are functional, code is ready for live demonstration, and any required 
software/hardware is set up in advance. 
Review Process 
● 
Each team is allotted 20–25 minutes in total: 10–15 minutes for presentation and 5–10 minutes for Q&A and 
demonstration. 
● 
The presentation must cover: problem statement, objectives, methodology, architecture/design, live 
demonstration, and outcomes with future scope. 
● 
Grading must reflect technical depth, execution quality, learning outcomes, and practical applicability. 
Post-Review Responsibilities 
● 
Marks awarded by the examiner must be recorded and signed by all panel members. 
● 
Constructive feedback should be given to each team, verbally or in writing. 
● 
Final hardbound reports (with signatures) must be submitted for departmental archiving.
